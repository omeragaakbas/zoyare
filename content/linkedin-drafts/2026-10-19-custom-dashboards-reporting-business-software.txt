Every company I talk to has the same problem: the data is there, but getting a clear view of it is a weekly ordeal.

Someone compiles a report in Excel. Half the figures are outdated by Tuesday. Management makes decisions on numbers that are already wrong.

The reflex is to add "a dashboard." But which kind?

Here's the distinction nobody explains clearly:

→ Analytical BI (Power BI, Tableau, Metabase) is for exploration. Slicing revenue, building ad-hoc reports, drilling down. These tools are excellent — and for most analysis use cases, building custom is a waste of money.

→ Operational dashboards are different. The warehouse screen showing today's pick queue. The support team's live ticket count. Data embedded in the workflow, updated in seconds, no separate login. For this, a custom build almost always wins.

The expensive mistake: using an analytical tool for operational needs (slow, wrong UX), or building custom when a BI tool would work in a week.

The hidden cost nobody budgets for: data preparation. In most projects, 40–60% of the time goes into getting the data into a consistent, queryable state — not building the charts themselves.

Before you start any reporting project, answer these five questions:
1. Who are the users, and what decisions do they make?
2. Where does the data live, and is it consistent?
3. How fresh does it need to be?
4. Can this data go to an external service? (GDPR matters here)
5. Does it need to be embedded in your software, or can it be a separate tool?

Get those five answers right and the build-vs-buy decision usually becomes obvious.
