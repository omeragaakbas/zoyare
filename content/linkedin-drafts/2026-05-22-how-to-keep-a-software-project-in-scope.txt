The average software project exceeds its budget by 45% and its timeline by 50%.

A project starts at €30,000 and 10 weeks. It finishes at €48,000 and 16 weeks. Nobody is happy — even though the product works.

Scope creep isn't caused by bad intentions. It's caused by learning things during development that nobody knew at the start. The question isn't how to prevent all changes — it's how to manage them.

Six things that actually work:

1. Start with a real discovery phase (20–30% less rework)
2. Document scope as a living checklist, not a 40-page spec
3. Make every change request explicit: "This adds Y hours and delays Z days. Still want it?"
4. Build in phases — ship v1, learn, then plan v2
5. Lock sprint scope once it starts
6. Budget 15–20% contingency as an explicit line item

The biggest lever is on the client side: one decision-maker, show up to every sprint review, and trust that "great idea, but that's v2" is the fastest path to a working product.
