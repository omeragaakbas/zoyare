A client once asked me: "Is it tested?" I said yes — and then realised that answer means almost nothing without context.

Here's what I've seen happen on projects where testing was deferred to the end:

The bug found two days before launch? Two hours to fix. The same bug found two months after launch — when it had quietly wrong'd every invoice that went out? Weeks of damage control.

IBM published this math decades ago: a bug found during development costs 1x to fix. Found in production: 10–15x. The numbers hold up.

What actually makes the difference:
— Tests written alongside the code, not in a dedicated "QA sprint" at the end
— A staging environment ready from week two, not the day before go-live
— A CI pipeline that runs the full suite on every commit, so problems surface immediately

The testing budget on a typical project is 15–25% of build time. On a €40,000 project, that's €6,000–€10,000. Skipping it tends to cost €15,000–€40,000 over the following two years in fixes, support and eventual refactoring.

It's also the question most people don't ask when evaluating a quote: "What does the test coverage look like at handover?"

Worth asking before you sign.
