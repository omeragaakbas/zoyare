"If you fell out with your software supplier tomorrow, could you walk away with everything you need?"

Most business owners never ask this — until they're mid-dispute and discover their own domain is registered under their developer's personal email.

Vendor lock-in usually isn't malicious. It's just sloppiness that quietly takes your choices away. It hides in four places:

→ The code. You paid for it, but you don't actually have it — or nobody else can run it.
→ The data. Easy to get in, a nightmare to export.
→ The hosting. Domains and accounts in the supplier's name, not yours.
→ The knowledge. One person understands how it works.

The fix costs almost nothing upfront. Five questions settle it before you ever sign:

1. Do we own the IP to the code outright — in writing?
2. Does the source live in OUR repository?
3. Are hosting, domain and accounts in our name?
4. Can we export all our data ourselves, anytime?
5. What does a handover to another developer cost?

A good partner answers all five without flinching. The whole point of doing the work well is that you COULD leave — you just won't want to.
