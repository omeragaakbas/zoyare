Everyone sells low-code as faster and cheaper. Sometimes it is. Often it isn't.

I've built production software on Mendix — including a BuildingX connector for Siemens. So this isn't a low-code-is-bad take. It's about knowing where the platform earns its keep and where it quietly works against you.

The part nobody mentions: licensing. A custom app is a one-time build plus cheap hosting. Low-code adds a subscription you pay every year, scaling with apps and users. Over five years it often overtakes the cost of building custom outright.

My rule of thumb:
→ Short-lived internal tool, standard screens? Low-code, build it fast.
→ Long-lived, distinctive product? Custom — before the licence dwarfs the build.

Before you choose, do one piece of math: what does five years of licensing cost versus building it once? It flips the decision more often than people expect.
