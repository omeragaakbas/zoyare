August 2026 was the deadline most Dutch businesses missed.

That was when the EU AI Act's high-risk AI requirements came into full effect. If your software uses AI for HR decisions, credit scoring, or any other high-stakes use case — those rules now apply to you.

The good news: most business software with AI features is NOT high-risk.

Document extraction, support ticket routing, invoice processing, automated reporting — these typically fall into the "limited risk" category. The main obligation there is transparency: tell users when they're interacting with AI. That's it.

Where Dutch companies get caught:
→ HR tools that use AI to rank or score candidates
→ Systems that use AI for credit or financial risk assessments
→ Any use of a third-party AI API without an enterprise data processing agreement

That last one is the most common gap I see. Using the OpenAI or Claude API on standard consumer terms is not GDPR-compliant if you're sending personal data through it. You need the enterprise agreement.

Four questions worth answering this week:
1. What risk tier is our AI use case?
2. Do we have enterprise API terms with our AI provider?
3. Can a human override AI outputs without calling a developer?
4. Do we have logging that lets us audit AI decisions?
