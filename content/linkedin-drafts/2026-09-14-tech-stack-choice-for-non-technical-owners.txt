One question comes up in almost every first call: "Does it matter what technology you use?"

The honest answer: the technology choice rarely determines whether the software works. But it does determine two things that matter a lot to you as a business owner:

1. How easy it is to find someone else to maintain it in three years
2. How deeply you're locked into whoever built it

PHP and JavaScript have massive talent pools — tens of thousands of developers in the Netherlands alone. A "custom framework" the studio built themselves? That talent pool might be twelve people.

When your developer leaves, raises their rates, or simply isn't available — and at some point, they will be — the tech stack decides whether you're free to move on or stuck negotiating from weakness.

Four questions worth asking before you approve any tech proposal:
→ Why this stack over the alternatives?
→ How many developers in the Netherlands know it well?
→ Is the framework actively maintained?
→ If you were unavailable, how long would it take another developer to get up to speed?

A good developer using a good stack answers all four calmly and concretely. Vagueness here is the signal that matters.
