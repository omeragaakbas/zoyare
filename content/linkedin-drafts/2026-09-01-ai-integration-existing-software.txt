Most "we should do something with AI" projects fail for the same reason: nobody picked a process first.

After a year of these conversations, almost all the value in existing business software sits in three patterns:

1. Reading unstructured input - invoices, supplier PDFs, scanned forms, free-text tickets. Someone reads them and types the contents somewhere else. This is the highest-return AI integration in most businesses and the least exciting one.

2. Routing and classification - which department, urgent or not, fast track or not. Decisions that are cheap individually and eat a full day in aggregate.

3. Search over your own material - "what did we agree with this client about delivery times". Works well, provided the documents are findable. If they live across five systems, that is the project.

What it costs: €8k-25k to build a well-scoped integration into existing software. Running cost is usually a few hundred euros a month - the expense people forget to budget is human review, and you do need it.

The comparison that matters isn't "what does AI cost". It's what the manual step costs now. Two hours a day of copying data is roughly €25k a year. Two hours a month is never worth automating, and anyone telling you otherwise is selling something.

Before connecting anything, four questions: where does the data physically go, what happens on the 3% it gets wrong, can you measure whether it's working, and can you switch providers without a migration project.
